## What changed

The judge now sees the original question. Reviewed reports/pages are non-exhaustive references: the same fact may be supported elsewhere in the active corpus, so exact page matching or citing every listed source is not required. Material values, currency, units, signs, fiscal periods, and GAAP/non-GAAP distinctions still matter. Explicit quotation/source requests remain binding. Citations, explanations, formulas, and comparison operands are not required unless requested; a correct bare number or company name can be a complete answer. Clarifications/refusals do not require financial figures or report citations.

Formatting has its own deterministic `format_compliance` check. It full-matches the entire response after trimming surrounding whitespace. A correct value with added prose or citations can pass quality and fail formatting; a wrong value with the right shape can pass formatting and fail quality. Existing cases have three checks; cases with an output pattern have four. Strict pass requires every applicable check. No provider output is stripped of citations, rewritten, or otherwise postprocessed to make these tests pass.

The previous 104 CSV cases and all their existing field values are preserved exactly. FMT01–FMT10 add plain numbers, millions/billions conversions, fixed decimal precision, a leading negative sign, and company-name-only comparisons. See [evaluation-changes.md](baseline-2026-11-02-v2/evaluation-changes.md) and [revised-rubric.txt](baseline-2026-11-02-v2/revised-rubric.txt) for the exact contract.

**No agent, tool, or provider source was modified during this revision.** Their pre-run and post-run SHA-256 hashes match. The model and retrieval settings also remain unchanged. Existing discovery/trajectory requirements were preserved. The earlier baseline had already been changed by a separate coverage-discovery refactor before this request; that matters when comparing old results.

## Main findings and remaining judge limits

- The strongest strict tags were out-of-distribution and multiple-company questions (both 10/10), followed by tool usage (13/14) and calculations (9/10). Formatting was weakest (0/10); image questions remained at 5/10. These tags have small samples and measure different requirements.
- Correct source-equivalent answers now pass examples such as ACC01, ACC03, and ACC04. Wrong values are still rejected: IMG02 again substitutes 41.9% for the expected 31.6% Q4 operating margin; IMG05 reports AWS/TTM chart values rather than the requested consolidated quarterly values. TOOL08, IF03, and IMG09 still miss requested evidence or incorrectly report it unavailable.
- All ten formatting checks fail. FMT03–FMT08 and FMT10 pass factual quality but include formulas, units, or citation text. FMT01 asks permission to include a citation instead of returning the number. FMT02 incorrectly treats Amazon's 2024 comparative net income as unavailable because its standalone 2024 filing is outside coverage; the retained 2025 filing contains the needed comparative figure.
- **The judge is still imperfect.** FMT09 outputs the correct winner, Alphabet, with citations, but the judge fails factual quality solely for the extra citation text despite the rubric separating format compliance. Official counts remain 7/10 quality and 0/10 formatting; no score was manually changed. The other seven correct-content/incorrect-format cases are distinguished correctly.
- Conversely, ACC02, ACC05, ACC06, ACC10, IMG07, and TAB10 receive quality passes for clarifications even though their reviewed expectations request factual answers. Their workflow checks fail, so they do not become strict successes. The 98 quality passes are model judgments, not 98 independently verified complete answers. A later rubric revision should restrict clarification/refusal acceptance to cases where that behavior is actually expected.
- Seven formatting cases would have passed the other three checks. The new format criterion therefore reduces the all 114-case strict count from 91 to 84; all ten format failures remain visible, including those that also fail another criterion.

## Recommendations for a later iteration — not implemented

- Review formatting failures against the current answer prompt's requirement to cite factual claims. Decide how user-requested bare values/company names should coexist with citations retained in metadata. No prompt or output-postprocessing change was made here.
- Review unsupported-company selections that can raise a verified-coverage error instead of producing an unavailable-data response. Keep such runtime errors in the benchmark until a separately authorized agent fix is evaluated.
- Use the per-case quality and workflow failures to target remaining evidence-recovery, calculation, scope, or ambiguity issues. Accepting equivalent source locations does not excuse wrong values, periods, signs, or fabricated evidence.
- Manually audit representative judge passes and failures. The judge sees the reviewed answers, references, annotations, question, and output; it does not independently read every cited PDF page or prove citation entailment. Relaxed citation-location requirements improve fairness but do not establish complete grounding.
- For a causal comparison of grader changes, regrade the same frozen outputs under both rubrics in a separate experiment. For agent improvements, hold the suite/rubric constant and report factual quality, workflow, formatting, latency, and cost separately.

The evaluation itself ran for **18 min 41 s** at four-case concurrency. The original 104 cases passed 84/104; the original 100 subset passed 80/100, and the four coverage cases passed 4/4. Source/page hit flags in trajectories.json are diagnostic reference matches only and do not participate in pass/fail decisions.
